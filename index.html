About Me

Data & Machine Learning

Responsible Credit Risk Modelling for Digital Micro-Lending

Vo Phan Vuong Hung  |  ECON1612 Big Data, Machine Learning and Society  |  2025

Context

This project examined how machine learning could be used to predict default risk in a hypothetical Vietnamese digital lending environment. The scenario required considering the real-world consequences of automated credit decisions for both lenders and borrowers.

My Approach

I cleaned and preprocessed the dataset, explored default patterns, developed classification models, evaluated model performance using multiple metrics, analysed feature importance, and investigated fairness across customer groups.

What I Learned

Working with this dataset changed my understanding of what it means to apply machine learning to financial problems. Model performance alone was not sufficient; the assignment required me to consider false negatives, fairness across groups and the consequences of automated lending decisions. This made me recognise that quantitative tools need to be evaluated not only for predictive accuracy but also for their suitability and consequences within the decision context.

Capabilities Demonstrated

#AoL4 Digitally Adept: Python, Jupyter, ML models, data preprocessing.
#AoL5 Expert: Applied disciplinary knowledge (finance + risk) to a real decision problem.

Career Relevance

This is my strongest artefact connecting financial decision-making with quantitative and technological methods. It demonstrates capability relevant to credit analysis, risk analytics, and responsible lending, all of which sit at the intersection of finance and technology.

Data & Machine Learning

Big Data, Causality and Prediction in Vietnam's Energy Sector

Vo Phan Vuong Hung  |  ECON1612 Big Data, Machine Learning and Society  |  2025

Context

Using Vietnam Electricity (EVN) as the business context, this assignment examined how to distinguish between causal policy questions and predictive operational questions, and determine which analytical approach suits each.

My Approach

I formulated causal questions, predictive questions, evaluated econometric vs. ML approaches, identified potential data sources, assessed feasibility, and considered ethical implications of large-scale data use in a state-owned energy context.

What I Learned

This work changed my understanding of analytical problem-solving by showing me that the method should follow the decision problem rather than the other way around. Earlier in my studies, my understanding of data analysis was largely focused on selecting methods and producing results. Through this project, I began to recognise that the quality of an analysis also depends on defining the decision problem correctly.

Career Relevance

Demonstrates analytical thinking relevant to economics consulting, policy advisory, and data strategy roles where selecting the right analytical framework matters more than simply running models.

Econometrics

Governance, Institutions and Economic Growth

Vo Phan Vuong Hung  |  ECON1313 Basic Econometrics  |  2024

Context

This project examined the relationship between governance quality, foreign direct investment, natural-resource rents and GDP per capita using macroeconomic data and econometric modelling.

My Approach

I conducted a literature review, prepared macroeconomic data, performed descriptive statistics and correlations, developed OLS regression models with interaction terms, conducted hypothesis testing, and discussed policy implications and model limitations.

What I Learned

This project strengthened my ability to translate an economic question into an empirical model and critically evaluate the assumptions and limitations behind quantitative results. It demonstrated the progression from describing economic relationships to statistically testing them.

Career Relevance

Demonstrates the economics side of my profile: the ability to formulate research questions, work with macroeconomic data, and apply econometric modelling to policy-relevant problems.
